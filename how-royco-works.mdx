#### The Tranching Mechanism

Every Royco market begins with a single strategy base asset. Capital from depositors is routed into that source, with contracts governing how returns and losses flow back to the different tranches.

The fundamental mechanism: **JT capital provides first-loss coverage for Senior capital, while LT provides liquidity.** The protocol enforces a minimum amount of JT capital (the Coverage Ratio) and LT capital (the Protection Ratio), respectively, that must stand behind every dollar of Senior deposits. If this ratio is not met, new Senior deposits are not accepted and yield is redirected to them.

Both ST and JT capital are deployed into the same base asset. JT is not sitting in a separate reserve; it is co-invested alongside ST, earning the base yield from the same source. The difference is that JT also earns a risk premium for providing first-loss coverage, and in exchange, JT absorbs losses immediately. Any drawdown reduces JT's capital before ST is affected.

LT is not directly deployed into the same base asset. Instead, it is deployed into a Balancer Pool Token in a rate-scaled AMM pool made up of ST tokens and a stablecoin. ST holders can sell into this pool at any time, with the resulting price discrepancies creating arbitrage opportunities that maintain the pool’s peg.

JT and LT are co-invested in varying degrees to the same base asset as ST.

**An Example**

Suppose a market is deployed on top of a USDC lending strategy yielding 8% annualized:

|  | Deposits | APY | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Senior (ST) | \$8,000,000 | ~5% | Base yield minus premium paid to JT for coverage |
| Junior (JT) | \$2,000,000 | ~13.5% | Base yield plus premium for first-loss coverage |
| Liquidity (LT) | \$2,000,000 | ~11% | Pool Asset Yield plus premium for liquidity provision and trading fees, minus impermanent loss |
| Total | \$12,000,000 |  |  |
| Coverage | 20% |  | \$2M JT / \$10M JT \+ ST |
| Liquidity | 25% |  | \$2M LT / \$8M LT |

Coverage is measured on the JT \+ ST pool: \$2M JT / \$10M (JT \+ ST) = 20% coverage. Coverage of 20% means JT can absorb up to a 20% drawdown on the \$10M pool before ST is affected.

Liquidity is measured against Senior: \$2M LT / \$8M ST = 25% liquidity. This sizes the LT's market-making depth to an amount equivalent to 25% of Senior's value, in the pool as exit depth behind Senior shares.

JT absorbs any losses immediately. ST and LT are only exposed once JT's entire balance is consumed. Because the LT's assets are partially composed of ST shares, its loss exposure mirrors that of the Senior tranche.

**Unlike JT and ST, LT losses are path-dependent.** During a drawdown, ST holders sell into the pool, raising LT's exposure to the senior asset. If the market recovers, this creates a profit: the LT bought discounted ST shares that recover to full value. However, in a realized drawdown, the LT suffers losses because it actively converted stablecoins into devalued ST shares.

#### Coverage Requirements

Royco enforces a **minimum coverage requirement** for each market. Coverage represents the maximum percentage drawdown the underlying can experience before ST capital is affected. It is a hard constraint: if coverage falls below the minimum, new ST deposits and Junior withdrawals are paused until JT capital returns to the required level.

ST depositors are shielded by the JT buffer, and are able to freely exit via the LT. The minimum buffer is smart-contract-enforced, so Seniors always know the floor backing their position.

**Coverage minimums are set per market** by external risk curators**.** The minimum coverage requirement is calibrated individually for each market based on its individual risk factors. 

#### Liquidity Requirements

Royco also enforces a **minimum liquidity requirement** for markets with liquidity functionality enabled. The liquidity requirement reflects the size of the AMM pool available for ST to exit into. If liquidity falls below the minimum, new ST deposits and LT withdrawals will be paused, similar to the coverage mechanism.

#### How Yield Is Split

In every Royco market, the base asset generates returns. The **Yield Distribution Model (YDM)** decides how those returns are divided between the tranches.

JT provides first-loss protection to ST, and LT provides liquidity. In exchange, JT and LT earn premiums; a share of Senior's yield. The YDM determines the size of that premium based on supply and demand.

The core idea: when JT or LT capital is scarce, their respective pool earns more. When capital is plentiful, the JT and LT earn less. This happens automatically with no governance votes or manual adjustments.

**What Drives the Split: Understanding Utilization**

<video src="/videos/chapter-03-utilization-1.mp4" controls />

The YDM looks at one number: **utilization.** Utilization measures how much capacity is currently being used to back the market. Utilization is used for both coverage and liquidity. Understanding utilization requires understanding how each of these mechanisms work:

**Coverage** is the percentage of the total pool that JT represents. It represents the maximum drawdown JT can absorb before ST is affected. For instance, \$2M of JT capital backing \$8M of ST capital gives 20% coverage, meaning JT can absorb a 20% loss on the pool without ST capital being touched.

**Liquidity** is the depth of the LT relative to the ST. Instead of waiting for primary redemption, Seniors can sell shares into the LT pool, up to the liquidity available there.

**Utilization measures how close a supporting tranche is to its respective floor**; coverage for the JT and liquidity for the LT. It is the signal that drives the premium model in the protocol. At 40% utilization, a tranche holds far more than its required minimum. At 100% utilization, it has hit its required minimum, disabling both Senior deposits and either JT or LT withdrawals, depending which tranche is being utilized. At the 90% target, the tranche is 90% of the way between its maximum and the floor. The market is efficiently deployed but still has room to accept new capital.

Put simply: **coverage and liquidity tell you what a tranche provides; utilization tells you how efficiently that capacity is being used.**

**The Yield Curve**

<video src="/videos/chapter-04-premium-curve-2.mp4" controls />

Each market sets two yield curves. One sets JT's risk premium from coverage utilization, the other sets the LT's liquidity premium from liquidity utilization. Each curve is defined by three points:

> - Y0: at 0% utilization
> - YT: at 90% (the target)
> - Y100: at 100%, the maximum spend

Each curve is bounded by a maximum spend, expressed as a % of Senior yield. The two curves' maximums combined never exceed 100% of Senior yield.

**Below 90%: essentially flat.** Y0 and YT are close together. When JT or LT capital is plentiful, excess supply already dilutes their returns.

**Above 90%: steep.** Y100 is typically set notably higher than YT , meaning that as capital becomes scarce, the yield share ramps aggressively to attract new depositors. Y100 is set per market such that at 100% utilization, the resulting JT or LT yield is attractive enough to draw in new capital.

**Self-Adapting Yield Curve Over Time**

These three points don't stay fixed, as the curve adapts to market conditions over time. When a tranche's utilization consistently runs above target, its curve shifts up to offer a bigger premium and attract more capital. When utilization consistently sits below target, the curve shifts down. The further from 90%, the faster the adjustment. These shifts happen gradually, over days or weeks.

The result is a self-balancing system. If JT or LT capital leaves, utilization rises for that tranche and the premium increases, attracting new capital. If ST capital leaves, the opposite happens. The market continuously finds its own equilibrium.

**What Depositors See**

The APY shown on the interface is the trailing APY, the annualized rate from actual yield accrued over the stated duration. If utilization shifts or capital enters or exits the pool, future yields will differ.

#### Observation Period

Yield sources sometimes experience short-term drawdowns; temporary dislocations, oracle lag, or brief liquidity events that reverse within hours or days. The Observation Period lets the protocol observe whether a drawdown is real and sustained or just noise before allocating any loss to JT capital.

When a drawdown is detected by a market oracle, the protocol automatically enters the Observation Period. Seniors can no longer redeem directly via redemption, new JT deposits and LT redemptions are blocked, and 100% of yield is redirected to JT recovery, accelerating the rebuild of the first-loss buffer.

However, ST holders are not locked into their positions. The LT continues to provide secondary liquidity throughout the Observation Period, allowing ST holders to exit by selling ST shares into the LT AMM pool at the pool's current price.

ST deposits and JT redemptions also remain enabled, provided the market remains above minimum coverage. Any JT holder who executes withdrawal will realize drawdown losses at current NAV.

The protocol then observes a defined time window. If the strategy recovers within that window, no loss is realized. If the drawdown persists beyond the window, it is treated as a genuine loss and permanently allocated against JT capital. If the drawdown is severe enough to reach the market's Protected Exit Threshold (see below) before the window expires, JT losses are finalized immediately, and ST gain the option to withdraw.

**The Observation Period window is set per market.** Some markets may define a 7-day window; others 30 days; some markets may define a window of 0 days, in which case losses are finalized immediately upon detection with no observation period. The specific window for each market is displayed on its market page in the protocol interface.

#### Protected Exit

The **Protected Exit** allows ST depositors to safely withdraw their base assets at fair value when a market drawdown breaches a pre-configured threshold. If a strategy suffers a markdown that breaches this **Protected Exit Threshold**, the Observation Period is terminated and ST can withdraw their assets. This realizes losses against JT capital. Those who believe the remaining JT buffer is sufficient can choose to stay. If losses exhaust JT capital entirely, ST shares become directly exposed to further drawdown.

Senior's two exits are complementary: the LT pool gives an instant exit at the pool's current price, Protected Exit returns the underlying at fair value, but only once a drawdown breaches the threshold.

#### Protected Exit Bonus

Some markets may include an optional configuration called **Protected Exit Bonus**. When the Protected Exit Threshold is exceeded in a market with Protected Exit Bonus enabled, any Seniors who withdraws may receive a Bonus % (up to the market-defined %) for withdrawing. This is designed to assist ST in unwinding their positions, and help restore the market into a healthy state. The Bonus is sourced from remaining Junior capital.

**Example:** A market has Minimum Coverage = 10%, Protected Exit Threshold = 5% and Protected Exit Bonus = 2%. The base asset drops below 5% coverage remaining, breaching the threshold.

Alice (Senior, \$100k) chooses to exit. She receives her \$100k back plus a \$2k bonus from Junior capital: a reward for helping restore the market to a healthy state. Seniors who stay forgo the bonus.
