#### The Tranching Mechanism

Every Royco market begins with a single underlying yield source. The protocol deploys capital from depositors into that source and governs how returns and losses flow back to the different tranches.

The fundamental mechanism: **JT capital provides first-loss coverage for Senior capital, while LT provides exit liquidity.** The protocol enforces both a coverage and protection ratio; the minimum amount of JT and LT capital, respectively, that must stand behind every dollar of Senior deposits. If this ratio is not met, new Senior deposits are not accepted.

Both ST and JT capital are deployed into the same underlying strategy. JT is not sitting in a separate reserve; it is co-invested alongside ST, earning the base yield from the same source. The difference is that JT also earns a risk premium for providing first-loss coverage, and in exchange, JT absorbs losses immediately. Any drawdown reduces JT's capital before ST is affected.

LT is not directly deployed into the same underlying strategy, instead it is deployed into a Balancer Pool Token in a rate-scaled AMM pool made up of ST tokens and a stablecoin.

JT and LT are co-invested in varying degrees to the same underlying strategy as ST. This means that the coverage and liquidity layers are real, active capital that is earning yield, making the structure capital-efficient for all parties.

**An Example**

Suppose a market is deployed on top of a USDC lending strategy yielding 8% annualized. Both Senior and Junior capital are co-invested in the same strategy:

|  | Deposits | APY | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Senior (ST) | \$8,000,000 | ~5% | Base yield minus premium paid to Junior |
| Junior (JT) | \$2,000,000 | ~13.5% | Base yield plus premium for first-loss protection |
| Liquidity (LT) | \$2,000,000 | ~11% | Liquidity Premium \+ ST Appreciation \+ Trading Fees - Impermanent Loss |
| Total | \$12,000,000 |  |  |
| Coverage | 20% |  | \$2M JT / \$10M JT \+ ST |
| Liquidity | 20% |  | \$2M LT / \$10M LT \+ ST |

Coverage is measured on the JT \+ ST pool: \$2M JT / \$10M JT \+ ST = 20% coverage. Coverage of 20% means JT can absorb up to a 20% drawdown on the \$10M pool before ST is affected.

Likewise, Liquidity is measured on the LT \+ JT pool: \$2M LT / \$10M LT \+ ST = 20% coverage. Liquidity of 20% means the LT tranche is able to accommodate the exit of 20% of ST at current AMM pool size.

**Loss Example Scenarios**

| Drawdown | Loss on \$10M pool | Junior absorbs | Senior exposure | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5% | \$500K | \$500K (\$1.5M remaining) | \$0 | Junior reduced to \$1.5M. Senior unaffected. |
| 15% | \$1.5M | \$1.5M (\$500K remaining) | \$0 | Junior reduced to \$500K. Senior unaffected. |
| 25% | \$2.5M | \$2M (\$0 remaining) | \$500K | Junior fully consumed. Senior reduced to \$7.5M. |

JT absorbs losses immediately, from the first dollar. ST, and the ST shares held by the LT, are only exposed once JT's entire balance is consumed. Because the LT's assets are partially composed of ST shares, its loss exposure tracks Senior's, in step with direct ST holders.

**Unlike JT and ST, LT losses are path-dependent.** As the standing counterparty for ST exits, the LT accumulates ST shares whenever holders sell into the pool, raising its exposure to the underlying asset. When a drawdown reverts, this creates a profit: the LT buys discounted ST shares that recover to full value. When a drawdown is permanent and Seniors exit, it is a loss beyond the passive markdown: the LT converts stablecoins into impaired inventory.

#### Coverage Requirements

Royco enforces a **minimum coverage requirement** for each market. Coverage represents the maximum percentage drawdown the underlying can experience before ST capital is affected. It is a hard constraint: if coverage falls below the minimum, new ST deposits and Junior withdrawals are paused until JT capital returns to the required level.

ST depositors are shielded by the JT buffer, and are able to freely withdraw liquidity via the LT. The minimum buffer is smart-contract-enforced, so Seniors always know the floor backing their position.

**Coverage minimums are set per market.** The minimum coverage requirement is calibrated individually for each market based on several factors, including but not limited to: the risk profile and historical volatility of the underlying yield source, the asset class (lending, staking, tokenized RWA), the liquidity depth of the strategy, and the overall risk tolerance of the Senior vault (if it is a participant in the market). A lending market backed by a blue-chip protocol may carry a lower minimum than a newer yield source with a shorter track record. This per-market calibration ensures the coverage floor reflects the profile of the underlying source rather than a one-size-fits-all threshold.

#### Liquidity Requirements

Royco also enforces a **minimum liquidity requirement** for markets with liquidity functionality enabled. The liquidity requirement reflects the size of the secondary pool available for ST to exit into. If liquidity falls below the minimum, new ST deposits and LT withdrawals will be paused, similar to the coverage mechanism.

#### How Yield Is Split

In every Royco market, the underlying yield source generates returns. The **Yield Distribution Model (YDM)** decides how those returns are divided between the tranches.

JT provides first-loss protection to ST, and LT provides liquidity. In exchange, JT and LT earn premiums; a share of Senior's yield. The YDM determines the size of that premium based on supply and demand.

The core idea: when JT or LT capital is scarce, their respective pool earns more. When capital is plentiful, the JT and LT earn less. This happens automatically with no governance votes or manual adjustments.

**What Drives the Split: Understanding Utilization**

The YDM looks at one number: **utilization.** Utilization measures how much capacity is currently being used to back the market. Utilization is used for both coverage and liquidity. Understanding utilization requires understanding how each of these mechanisms work:

**Coverage** is the percentage of the total pool that JT represents. It represents the maximum drawdown of the underlying yield source that JT can absorb before ST is affected. For instance, \$2M of JT capital backing \$8M of ST capital gives 20% coverage, meaning JT can absorb a 20% loss on the pool without touching ST capital.

**Each market has a minimum coverage requirement** (set per market as described above). JT capital must stay at or above this floor. If it falls below, new ST deposits and current JT withdrawals are blocked until a JT buffer is replenished.

**Liquidity** is the depth of the LT relative to the ST. It represents the secondary-market depth standing behind ST shares, the capacity to absorb ST holders who want to exit by selling into the pool rather than waiting for primary redemption. For instance, \$2M of LT capital backing an \$8M ST provides 20% liquidity.

**Markets with liquidity enabled have a minimum liquidity requirement**, set per market**. T**he Liquidity Tranche's depth must stay at or above this floor. If it falls below, new Senior deposits and current Liquidity Tranche withdrawals are blocked until liquidity is replenished.

**Utilization measures how close a supporting tranche is to its respective floor**; coverage for the JT and liquidity for the LT. It is the signal that drives the premium model in the protocol. At 40% utilization, a tranche holds far more than its required minimum, with plenty of cushion above the floor. At 100% utilization, it is exactly at the required minimum, disabling both Senior deposits and either JT or LT withdrawals, depending which tranche is being utilized. At the 90% target, the tranche is 90% of the way from 'maximum excess' to 'exactly at the floor.' The market is efficiently deployed but still has room to accept new capital.

Put simply: **coverage and liquidity tell you what a tranche provides; utilization tells you how efficiently that capacity is being used.** High utilization means a tranche is close to its required floor and depositors earn a higher premium to attract new capital. Low utilization means a tranche has built up excess above the floor, and its effective premium is lower.

**The Yield Curve**

<video src="/videos/chapter-04-premium-curve.mp4" controls />

Each market sets three values at creation that define yield share across utilization:

> - Y0: at 0% utilization
> - YT: at 90% (the target)
> - Y100: at 100%

**Below 90%: essentially flat.** Y0 and YT are close together. When JT or LT capital is plentiful, excess supply already dilutes their returns.

**Above 90%: steep.** Y100 is typically set notably higher than YT , meaning that as capital becomes scarce, the yield share ramps aggressively to attract new depositors. Y100 is set per market such that at 100% utilization, the resulting JT or LT yield is attractive enough to draw in new capital.

**Self-Adapting Yield Curve Over Time**

These three points don't stay fixed, as the curve adapts to market conditions over time. When a tranche's utilization consistently runs above target, its curve shifts up to offer a bigger premium and attract more capital. When utilization consistently sits below target, the curve shifts down. The further from 90%, the faster the adjustment. These shifts happen gradually, over days or weeks.

The result is a self-balancing system. If JT or LT capital leaves, utilization rises for that tranche and the premium increases, attracting new capital. If ST capital leaves, the opposite happens. The market continuously finds its own equilibrium.

**What Depositors See**

The APY shown on the interface is the trailing 24-hour APY, the annualized rate from actual yield accrued over the previous 24 hours. A 7-day average is shown alongside it for a smoother view. Both are backward-looking. If utilization shifts or capital enters or exits the pool, future yields will differ.

#### Observation Period

Yield sources sometimes experience short-term drawdowns; temporary dislocations, oracle lag, or brief liquidity events that reverse within hours or days. The Observation Period lets the protocol observe whether a drawdown is real and sustained or just noise before allocating any loss against JT capital.

When a drawdown is detected, the protocol enters the Observation Period. Seniors can no longer redeem directly via redemption, new JT deposits are blocked, and 100% of yield is redirected to JT, accelerating the rebuild of the first-loss buffer. 

However, ST holders are not locked into their positions. The LT continues to provide secondary liquidity throughout the Observation Period, allowing ST holders to exit by selling ST shares into the LT AMM pool at the pool's current price. 

ST deposits and JT redemptions also remain enabled, provided the market remains above minimum coverage. Any JT holder who executes withdrawal will realize impermanent loss at the point of execution. 

The protocol then observes a defined time window. If the strategy recovers within that window, no loss is realized. If the drawdown persists beyond the window, it is treated as a genuine loss and permanently allocated against JT capital. If the drawdown is severe enough to reach the market's Protected Exit Threshold (see below) before the window expires, JT losses are finalized immediately, and ST gain the option to withdraw.

**The Observation Period window is set per market.** Some markets may define a 7-day window; others 30 days; some markets may define a window of 0 days, in which case losses are finalized immediately upon detection with no observation period. The specific window for each market is displayed on its market page in the protocol interface.

#### Protected Exit

Each market provides ST with first-loss coverage against persistent drawdowns through a **Protected Exit Threshold**. If a drawdown is deep enough, the Observation Period is terminated and ST can withdraw the underlying asset when there is a predefined level of coverage remaining, realizing losses against JT capital. Those who believe the remaining JT buffer is sufficient can choose to stay. If losses exhaust JT capital entirely, ST shares become directly exposed to further drawdown.

Protected Exit is the backstop to secondary liquidity. The LT pool offers immediate exit at a market price, which during drawdowns may sit below NAV. The LT pool is also finite: once the pool's available liquidity is exhausted, it can no longer provide an exit. Protected Exit serves the ST shares the pool cannot: those unwilling to sell at the pool's discounted price and willing to wait for the threshold, and those who reach the pool only to find its liquidity drained. The two exits are complementary: the LT pool gives speed at a market-set price, Protected Exit gives the underlying at a finalized loss once the threshold is breached.

#### Protected Exit Bonus

Some markets may include an optional configuration called **Protected Exit Bonus**. When the Protected Exit Threshold is exceeded in a market with Protected Exit Bonus enabled, any Seniors who optionally withdraw may additionally receive a small Bonus % (up to the market-defined %) for withdrawing. This is designed as a minor incentive for ST to withdraw into the underlying asset, which assists in restoring the market into a healthy state. The Bonus is sourced from remaining Junior capital.

**Example:** A market has Minimum Coverage = 10%, Protected Exit Threshold = 5% and Protected Exit Bonus = 2%. The underlying drops below 5% coverage remaining, breaching the threshold.

Alice (Senior, \$100k) chooses to exit. She receives her \$100k back plus a \$2k bonus from Junior capital: a reward for helping restore the market to a healthy state. Seniors who stay forgo the bonus.
